**Perfection. Perfection. Perfection.**
When I was younger I praised myself for being a perfectionist. I thought it was a truly special, also beautiful thing about me to consider; I do not do many but which I do will truly hit the mark. 

Except that I had nothing I could say I really did. And years later, I can see and understand the underlying thoughts and how it all related. 

"You mustn't start, as when you do, you have to invest too much into it and that won't do."

"I am not finished, I am not yet in awe. When can I finish? I cannot."

And I am happy to say, that one of my early and conscious decisions amongst others was to ditch that whole concept. 
I am not exactly sure where I found this truth, yet I can tell you about it, I will wait for someone to disagree: Nothing will ever be perfect. Nothing ever was perfect. To truly come close to perfection, you must spend an unsufferable amount of time. Call it mastery. 

In detail. I differentiate between two sights of "perfection", one entails an illusion, the other a harsh truth. 

**Illusion.** Smooth it out. Smother it with oil. Smooth it out until it is so smooth you barely feel the surface so light is the touch to it. You have reached a perfectly fine texture, every atom must be aligned next to it neighbour- yet you look with your magnifying glass, or even worse, with a microscope, suddenly what you were sure to be the most perfect glazing surface finish you ever saw is.. as unsmooth as a mountain range?? I think you might get the gist. 
I call it an illusion, even though it really doesn't have to be that. But it is the most important reminder. If you ever work something like this, you must consider the level of observance you want your work to be taken in and understand the means and time that will be needed for this then. 

This is suddenly very insightful, if you look at skilled paintings. Some are perfect, fine in every detail surely, but the truly striking ones in my opinion are those, which are somewhat foggy when you look closely, almost not being able to understand the motive even, then when you step back - *magic*. A complete and emotional scene unfolds in a sudden depth. Brushmarks which were made as if the painter did not even bother, yet they fall perfectly where they belong. Because they know where they can be broad and where the must set clear strokes. 

This runs nicely to the second part of perfections:
**Harsh Truths.** How was the painter able to achieve producing artwork, which understood so perfectly where to balance carelessness with carefulness, such that it is not overdetailed and too strict, yet clear in motif, dimension, colour? Whenever someone displays skillfulness with utter ease, people call it "talent", "must be born with it", and the likes. It is truly disheartening, that this is the general wisdom of the people about why individuals can do things they cannot. There are surely cases which even boggle myself, watching these chinese 6 year olds play high-level violin sonata's with speed, vibration yet no error, or also the general topic of how genes influence us also has a say. 
But if you do believe, someone outdoes you because of their talent, then I must wholeheartedly disagree and tell you the harsh truth finally: you are afraid of taking responsibility of your own potential.

Because the harsh truth is, what seems to be perfect can exist, it just takes an incredible amount of time and effort to reach it. And so the harshest truth is, you must decide how much you really want it, you must understand how hollow your life may be if it was absent, you must know what this "perfection" is worth to you. 

And this is exactly what hit me when I dumped my perfectionism concept out of my head; it was when I saw myself practising the same thing again and again and again and again and again and... 
And sometimes I would also do that, I would to it again and again and.. yeah yeah you know, but I would not do it to a stage of "true perfection" (what even is that lol), but here I learned another insight. I spent a good time of practise, yet would have to spend probably the same amount again *at least* to achieve flawlessness somewhat. But having gone there halfway, I had improved so much in this skill, that I could enjoy doing it for the sake of it, not always grinding for getting better at it. And I also had improved so much, that I could try the next harder thing from it, if I wanted to. 

Example, playing the violin, I play the same passage over and over again, it sounds like shit. I must get it right! I must get it right! So I keep repeating it. And now, I don't get it right every time.. Maybe 3 out of 5 times. But even the 2 times I don't get it right, it at least does not sound completely like shit anymore. So I improved my general sound. And now, even though the passage is not *perfect*, it is good enough so that I can comfortably move on to the next one.

But the most interesting things happen later then, years after I ditch my perfectionalism. I can look back at some of my works which I did without thinking much about them, some of practised skill, some of mere random impulses, and I see myself in awe, my heart is shaken, my pupils widen. 
My work felt perfect. Some I remembered making, back then I did not like them, some I remembered and thought nothing about, some I did not even remember at all. 

Reflecting on all these thoughts together, I can deduce that perfection is a human invented concept. It occurs nowhere on earth but in our perception - it is an emotion. What may be perfect for you might be just a boring mess to someone else. It is when we can perceive the difference between what average may be able to accomplish and what you see in front of you. It is when you do not understand how this is supposed to be human anymore. It is many things, but it most certainly is seldomly visible to you, when you freshly produced something out of your own. 

A completely subjective experience, and one you will never see in real time coming out of your own hand, because you are not perfect and never will be. And it must be that way. Because not being perfect in one thing means being good in many. 

I thereby ask you, if you are the perfectionist, to ask yourself what it really is that you are seeking. What is the core of the craving you are trying to satisfy made of? Is it the need to feel proud of something, or the fear of failure? 

Fear you must confront, pride you must take off its high horse. Understand if you really want the skill/experience and if you are willing to pay up for it.
